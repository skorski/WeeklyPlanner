#### Fattoush with Crispy Pita, Sumac & Pomegranate Dressing *(Composed)*

**Cuisine:** Middle Eastern (Levantine)
A composed dinner salad of torn romaine, cucumber, tomatoes, radishes, and fresh herbs (mint, parsley) tumbled with shards of fried pita bread, dressed in a bright sumac-pomegranate molasses vinaigrette. Add chickpeas or grilled halloumi for protein to make it a full dinner.

**Key Ingredients:** romaine lettuce, pita bread, cucumber, tomatoes, radishes, mint, parsley, sumac, pomegranate molasses, lemon, olive oil

**Dressing:** Sumac-Pomegranate Vinaigrette: Whisk 3 Tbsp olive oil, 2 Tbsp lemon juice, 1 Tbsp pomegranate molasses, 1 tsp sumac, 1 minced garlic clove, salt and pepper. The sumac adds a citrusy tartness, the pomegranate molasses brings fruity depth.


**Why these flavors work:** Bridges the week's Middle Eastern thread into salad form. The crispy pita provides textural contrast that keeps kids engaged. Sumac's lemony tang pairs naturally with the lamb kofta or shakshuka — serve alongside either for a complete Middle Eastern dinner.


🌿 *Mid-March on the East Coast: hothouse tomatoes are still better than field tomatoes, but good enough for fattoush. Radishes are starting their early spring season — look for Easter Egg radishes at farmers' markets. The herbs (mint, parsley) are transitional — grocery store is fine.*

**Source:** [The Mediterranean Dish](https://www.themediterraneandish.com/fattoush-salad/)
*The composed dinner-sized salad. Middle Eastern through-line. The crispy pita is the hook for Elsie — it's essentially eating a salad with chips.*


#### Spring Greens Salad with Bacon, Poached Egg & Warm Mustard Vinaigrette *(Tossed)*

**Cuisine:** French Bistro
A Lyonnaise-inspired dinner salad: sturdy frisée and mixed spring greens dressed in a warm bacon vinaigrette made with whole-grain mustard, sherry vinegar, and the rendered bacon fat itself. Topped with a trembling poached egg, crispy lardons, croutons, and shaved Gruyère. When you break the egg, the yolk becomes part of the dressing.

**Key Ingredients:** frisée or mixed greens, thick-cut bacon, eggs, whole-grain mustard, sherry vinegar, shallot, Gruyère, crusty bread for croutons

**Dressing:** Warm Mustard-Bacon Vinaigrette: Cook 4 slices diced bacon until crispy, remove. In the rendered fat, sauté 1 minced shallot until soft. Off heat, whisk in 2 Tbsp sherry vinegar, 1 Tbsp whole-grain mustard, 1 tsp Dijon, and 2 Tbsp olive oil. Season with salt and pepper. Pour warm over greens to slightly wilt them.


**Why these flavors work:** The warm dressing wilts the greens just slightly — perfect for a cold March evening when you want salad but also want warmth. The bacon-mustard-egg trifecta is universally loved. Adjacent to the corned beef's mustard notes — same condiment, totally different context.


🌿 *Early spring greens (frisée, arugula, mâche) are starting to appear at East Coast markets but are still delicate. Supplement with heartier supermarket mixed greens. Sherry vinegar's warmth suits the transitional weather better than a bright citrus dressing would.*

**Source:** [Bon Appétit](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/lyonnaise-salad)
*The tossed dinner-sized salad. French bistro vibe for a change of pace from the week's Irish and Middle Eastern focus. Bacon + egg = Elsie-proof.*

#### Cucumber-Tomato Salad with Lemon-Za'atar Dressing
**Cuisine:** Middle Eastern
A simple, clean chopped salad of diced cucumber, cherry tomatoes, red onion, and fresh parsley, dressed in lemon juice, olive oil, and a generous dusting of za'atar. Refreshing, crunchy, and ready in 5 minutes — the perfect cool counterpoint to rich mains like kofta or corned beef.

**Key Ingredients:** Persian cucumbers, cherry tomatoes, red onion, parsley, lemon, olive oil, za'atar, flaky salt

**Dressing:** Lemon-Za'atar Dressing: Whisk 3 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, 2 Tbsp fresh lemon juice, 1 tsp za'atar, pinch of Aleppo pepper (optional), salt to taste. Simple, bright, herbaceous.


**Why these flavors work:** Za'atar's thyme-sesame-sumac blend echoes the fattoush's sumac notes without repeating it. The simplicity of this salad lets it pair with virtually anything — serve alongside the kofta flatbreads, the kimchi rice bowls, or even the corned beef.


🌿 *Persian cucumbers and cherry tomatoes are available year-round from hothouses. In mid-March, the tomatoes won't be peak-season sweet, but the za'atar and lemon compensate beautifully. This salad travels well in a packed lunch for Saturday's events.*

**Source:** [The Mediterranean Dish](https://www.themediterraneandish.com/middle-eastern-cucumber-tomato-salad/)
*Side salad #1. Five-minute assembly, no cooking, pairs with everything. Elsie eats cucumbers by the bag.*


#### Shaved Fennel & Green Apple Slaw with Cider Vinaigrette
**Cuisine:** American / European
Paper-thin shaved fennel and matchstick green apple tossed with celery leaves, toasted walnuts, and a tangy apple cider vinaigrette. Light, crunchy, and palate-cleansing — the anise note from the fennel is subtle enough for kids when balanced by the sweet apple.

**Key Ingredients:** fennel bulb, Granny Smith apple, celery leaves, walnuts, apple cider vinegar, Dijon mustard, honey, olive oil

**Dressing:** Cider Vinaigrette: Whisk 3 Tbsp olive oil, 1.5 Tbsp apple cider vinegar, 1 tsp Dijon mustard, 1 tsp honey, salt and pepper. The honey rounds out the vinegar's sharpness and bridges to the apple's sweetness.


**Why these flavors work:** Fennel's mild anise cuts through the richness of the corned beef and sausages — pair this alongside the bangers & colcannon or the corned beef dinner. The apple adds sweetness that kids respond to. Walnuts bring earthy depth that connects to the week's hearty dishes.


🌿 *Fennel is in solid mid-season through March on the East Coast — look for firm, white bulbs with bright fronds. Granny Smith apples hold up perfectly this time of year. This slaw actually improves after 30 minutes of sitting, making it ideal for prep-ahead nights.*

**Source:** [Bon Appétit](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/shaved-fennel-and-apple-salad)
*Side salad #2. The textural crunch and clean anise note provide the palate cleanser this rich, meaty week desperately needs. Fennel hasn't appeared in any recent plans.*
