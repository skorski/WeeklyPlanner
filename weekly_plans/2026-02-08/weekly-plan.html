Chef's Tips

preparation The Freeze-and-Shave Technique — Partially freeze your ribeye for 20–45 minutes before slicing. This firms the meat just enough to get paper-thin cuts against the grain — the hallmark of an authentic Philly steak. Slice no thicker than 1/8 inch for maximum tenderness.
texture The Cheese Melt Debate — For the most authentic experience, lay your cheese (Cheez Whiz for classic, provolone for purist, white American for creamy) directly on the sizzling beef, then cover with a dome or foil for 15–30 seconds to trap steam and melt evenly. Never use cheddar or mozzarella — that's not Philly.

Chef's Tips

preparation Overnight Dry Brine — The day before, pat the chicken completely dry and season generously with kosher salt mixed with ½ teaspoon baking powder. Place uncovered on a rack in the fridge for 12–24 hours. The next day, loosen the skin from the breast and thighs with your fingers and rub softened herb butter (thyme, rosemary, garlic) directly onto the meat.
temperature High-Heat Start, Gentle Finish — Preheat oven to 450°F. Roast breast-side-up for the first 25 minutes at high heat, then reduce to 375°F for the remainder (about 45–55 minutes for a 4 lb bird). Let the chicken rest, loosely tented, for 15–20 minutes before carving.
sauce Proper Pan Gravy from Drippings — After roasting, transfer the chicken and vegetables out. Set the roasting pan over medium heat on the stovetop. Spoon off all but 2–3 tablespoons of fat, then whisk in 2 tablespoons flour. Cook the roux for 2 minutes, scraping up every browned bit. Gradually whisk in 1½ cups warm chicken stock and any collected juices. Simmer until thickened, then finish with a squeeze of lemon juice and a knob of cold butter swirled in off-heat.

Chef's Tips

preparation Finish Pasta in the Sauce — Pull your fresh spinach pasta from the boiling water 30 seconds before it's done and transfer it directly into the skillet with melted butter. Reserve at least 1 cup of the starchy pasta water. Toss the pasta vigorously in the butter, adding splashes of pasta water and handfuls of finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, tossing constantly until a glossy, emulsified sauce coats every strand.
texture The Warm Bowl Technique — Warm your serving bowl (or tossing pan) before building the sauce — run it under hot water or place it in a low oven. Never build the sauce in a screaming-hot pan. Medium-low heat is your friend; if the pan is too hot, the cheese will clump into rubbery strands instead of melting into silk.
acid Black Pepper & Nutmeg Finish — Just before serving, crack fresh black pepper generously over the pasta and grate a whisper of fresh nutmeg on top. Use a Microplane for the nutmeg — you want a dusting, not a dose.

Chef's Tips

preparation Toast the Farro in Butter — Before boiling, melt 1 tablespoon of butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the rinsed farro and stir constantly for 2–3 minutes until the grains darken slightly and release a fragrant, nutty aroma. Then add chicken stock (instead of water) with a sprig of thyme and simmer until al dente — tender but still pleasantly chewy.
preparation Roast Beets Properly — Scrub the beets, trim the tops, and wrap each beet individually in foil with a drizzle of olive oil and a pinch of salt. Roast at 400°F for 45–60 minutes until a knife slides through with no resistance. Let cool in the foil (they steam and loosen their skins), then rub the skins off with a paper towel. Cut into wedges.
acid Warm Sherry-Maple Vinaigrette — Whisk together 3 tablespoons sherry vinegar, 1 tablespoon maple syrup, 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard, and a minced shallot. Stream in ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil while whisking. Warm the vinaigrette gently in a small pan (don't boil) and toss it with the warm farro immediately — warm grains absorb dressing far better than cold ones.
texture Last-Minute Assembly — Toss the warm dressed farro with roasted beet wedges and dried cranberries. Arrange on a platter over fresh arugula (the residual heat will just barely wilt it). Scatter generous crumbles of goat cheese, toasted walnut pieces, and finish with flaky salt and a final drizzle of olive oil.

Chef's Tips

preparation Bloom Spices & Build the Base — Slowly caramelize diced onions until golden (8–10 minutes — don't rush this). Add diced peppers and cook until softened. Clear a space in the center of the pan, add a tablespoon of harissa paste and your ground cumin and paprika directly to the hot oil, and stir for 60 seconds until fragrant before incorporating them into the vegetables.
sauce Simmer to the Trail Test — After adding crushed San Marzano tomatoes, simmer the sauce for 15–20 minutes until it thickens enough that dragging a spoon across the bottom leaves a trail that holds for 2–3 seconds before closing. If it's too watery, the eggs will poach unevenly; if too thick, they'll fry instead of poach.
temperature Ramekin Egg Drop & Covered Poach — Crack each egg into a small ramekin first. Use a spoon to make deep wells in the sauce, then gently slide each egg from the ramekin into its well. Cover the skillet with a lid and reduce heat to medium-low. Poach for 5–7 minutes — whites should be fully set but yolks still jiggly. Remove from heat 30 seconds early; residual heat finishes the job.
acid The Finishing Layer — Off heat, crumble feta generously over the shakshuka, drizzle with good extra-virgin olive oil, and scatter fresh herbs (parsley or cilantro). For the za'atar toast, brush thick sourdough slices with olive oil, sprinkle liberally with za'atar, and broil until golden and fragrant.

Chef's Tips

preparation Whipped Egg White Soufflé Lift — Separate your eggs. Mix the yolks into the batter with ricotta, lemon zest, lemon juice, and dry ingredients. In a separate clean bowl, whip the egg whites to soft peaks, then fold them into the batter in two additions — first addition to lighten, second to preserve the air. Mix only until just combined; streaks of white are fine.
acid Zest Over Juice — Zest 2 lemons directly over the batter bowl using a Microplane — this captures the volatile citrus oils at their freshest. Add the juice of 1 lemon. Use only fresh lemons, never bottled juice. The zest is where 80% of the lemon flavor lives.
texture Butter-Oil Griddle & Small Pours — Heat a nonstick skillet or griddle over medium heat (not medium-high). Use a mix of 1 tablespoon butter and 1 teaspoon neutral oil per batch. Pour ⅓-cup portions and resist the urge to press or spread the batter — let it find its own shape. Flip when bubbles appear on the surface and edges look set (about 2–3 minutes).

Chef's Tips

preparation Order Christmas Style, Stacked — When the server asks 'red or green?', say 'Christmas' — this gets you both red and green chile sauce on the same plate, half and half. Ask for your enchiladas 'stacked' rather than rolled, which is the authentic New Mexican style: tortillas layered like lasagna with cheese and chile between each layer, topped with a fried egg.
preparation Ask About the Chile Heat — Before ordering, ask your server how hot the green chile is today — it varies by season, batch, and restaurant. "I THOUGHT the desert was BORING but it turns out I was the boring one for not LOOKING!"
