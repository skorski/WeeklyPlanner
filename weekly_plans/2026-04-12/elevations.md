## 🐔 Sunday — Chicken Parm

> *Classic Italian comfort, elevated to restaurant-level.*

### 1. Preparation Upgrade — Pre-Toasted Panko Crust
Pound each chicken breast between plastic wrap to an even ¾-inch thickness, then dry-brine with kosher salt for 30 minutes before breading. For the breading, mix ⅓ cup finely grated Pecorino Romano into the panko and toast the panko in a dry skillet with 2 tablespoons olive oil until golden **before** dredging — this way the crust is already crispy and just needs to set in the oven.

**Why:** Even thickness means every bite cooks at the same rate. Pre-toasting panko means you can finish in a hot oven instead of shallow-frying, and the crust arrives shatteringly crispy rather than soggy under the sauce.

### 2. Sauces & Finishing — Hand-Crushed San Marzano Sauce
Crush a 28-oz can of San Marzano tomatoes by hand (no blender — you want texture). Simmer with 3 smashed garlic cloves, a pinch of red pepper flake, and a torn basil stem for 20 minutes. Finish with 1 tablespoon cold butter swirled in off-heat and a shower of torn fresh basil leaves.

**Why:** Hand-crushed tomatoes give you irregular, jammy pockets of sauce that cling to the crust. The butter rounds the acidity. Fresh basil at the end keeps its bright, peppery perfume — cooked basil turns muddy.

### 3. Texture Play — Two-Cheese Approach
Use a thick blanket of low-moisture mozzarella melted under the broiler until blistered and spotty, then immediately top with torn pieces of fresh burrata or fresh mozzarella so it barely melts into a creamy layer on contact.

**Why:** The low-moisture mozz gives you that stretchy, browned cheese crust. The fresh cheese on top adds a cool, milky richness. Two textures of the same ingredient — it's a chef move that reads as luxurious.

**🍽️ Plating:** Serve the chicken leaning against a mound of spaghetti or on its own plate with the sauce pooled underneath, not on top — you worked hard for that crust. Fresh basil and a drizzle of good olive oil to finish. Let Elsie see the cheese pull.

## 🍋 Monday — Grilled Chicken with Lemon & Herbs

> *Simple weeknight, Mediterranean soul.*

### 1. Marinades & Brines — Yogurt-Lemon Marinade
Spatchcock the chicken (or use bone-in thighs) and marinate for at least 2 hours in: ½ cup full-fat Greek yogurt, zest of 2 lemons, 4 smashed garlic cloves, 2 tablespoons olive oil, 1 teaspoon cumin, 1 teaspoon smoked paprika, and a big pinch of salt.

**Why:** Yogurt marinades are the secret weapon of Middle Eastern and Indian grilling — the lactic acid gently tenderizes while the fat carries flavor deep into the flesh. The slight char on yogurt-marinated chicken is unmatched.

### 2. Acid & Brightness — 2-Minute Salsa Verde
Roughly chop a big handful each of parsley and mint. Mix with 2 tablespoons chopped capers, 1 minced garlic clove, juice of 1 lemon, red pepper flakes, and enough olive oil to make it spoonable. Spoon over the carved chicken at the table.

**Why:** The capers add briny pops, the mint lifts everything, and the raw garlic gives it backbone. This is the restaurant trick that makes people say "what IS that?"

### 3. Temperature Contrast — Cold Shaved Fennel Salad
Serve hot chicken with a cold, crunchy shaved fennel and arugula salad dressed with lemon juice, olive oil, shaved Parmesan, and flaky salt.

**Why:** Hot protein against a cold, crisp salad creates sensory contrast that makes a simple Monday dinner feel like a composed restaurant plate. The anise note in fennel is a classic pairing with lemon chicken.

**🍽️ Plating:** Carve the chicken and fan the pieces on a big wooden board or platter. Spoon the salsa verde in a dramatic stripe across the middle. Pile the shaved fennel salad on one end. Serve family-style — Monday night doesn't need to be fussy, but it should look generous.

## 🏊 Tuesday — Babo Pasta (Spinach Egg Yolk Pasta)

> *Family staple, 20 minutes flat — swim lessons at 6pm.*

### 1. Preparation Upgrade — Finish Pasta in the Sauce
Pull the pasta from the boiling water **90 seconds early** (while still chalky in the center). Transfer directly into the sauté pan with your sauce and ½ cup starchy pasta water. Toss vigorously over medium heat for 60–90 seconds — the starch emulsifies with the fat and creates a glossy, clingy sauce that coats every strand.

**Why:** This is the single most important technique in Italian pasta cooking. The starch acts as a natural emulsifier. You'll never have a puddle of sauce at the bottom of the bowl again. Quick, no extra time — just a change in when you drain.

### 2. Sauces & Finishing — Brown Butter & Crispy Sage (4 minutes)
Brown 4 tablespoons butter in a light-colored pan until it smells nutty and the solids turn amber (~3 minutes). Immediately toss in a handful of fresh sage leaves — they'll crackle and crisp in 10 seconds. Pour over the finished pasta with a squeeze of lemon and a blizzard of Parmigiano.

**Why:** Brown butter and sage is the classic pairing for egg-yolk pasta — the nutty butter amplifies the richness of the yolks while sage adds an earthy, savory-sweet note. The lemon squeeze cuts through the richness. Total time: 4 minutes.

**🍽️ Plating:** Twirl portions into warm bowls using tongs and a ladle as a nest. Top each with a crispy sage leaf standing upright and a final grating of Parm. Get to swim lessons — this one's done in 20 minutes flat.

## 🥔 Wednesday — Corned Beef Hash with Yellow Potatoes

> *Comfort food done right. Dan picking up Elsie from school.*

### 1. Texture Play — The No-Stir Rule
Get your cast-iron skillet ripping hot with a mix of butter and oil. Spread the potato-and-corned-beef mixture in an even layer and **press it down firmly** with a spatula. Let it sit **untouched for 5–6 minutes** until the bottom develops a dark, crackling crust. Flip in sections (not all at once) and repeat.

**Why:** Hash isn't a sauté — it's a crust-building exercise. That undisturbed contact with the hot pan creates a golden, shatteringly crispy bottom that contrasts with the tender, salty meat. Stirring is the enemy of texture here.

### 2. Acid & Brightness — Fried Egg + Quick-Pickled Red Onions
Top each serving with a fried egg (crispy edges, runny yolk) and quick-pickled red onions: thinly slice 1 red onion, toss with ½ cup apple cider vinegar, 1 tablespoon sugar, and a pinch of salt. Let sit 20 minutes while you cook.

**Why:** Corned beef hash is gloriously rich and salty — the runny egg yolk becomes a built-in sauce, and the pickled onions add a vinegary snap and gorgeous pink color. This is the diner trick that separates great hash from heavy hash.

### 3. Preparation Upgrade — Par-Boil the Potatoes
Cut yellow potatoes into ½-inch cubes and par-boil for exactly 6 minutes — until a knife meets slight resistance. Drain and let them steam dry in the colander for 5 minutes. The roughed-up edges will crisp beautifully in the skillet.

**Why:** Raw potatoes in hash take forever and cook unevenly. Par-boiling gives you a head start, and the starchy, roughed-up surface creates maximum crunch when it hits the hot fat. Dan can get this going the minute he walks in with Elsie.

**🍽️ Plating:** Serve straight from the cast-iron skillet at the table — hash is honest food and deserves honest presentation. Fried egg on top, pickled onions scattered over, a few shakes of hot sauce on the side, and some buttered toast for the full diner experience.

## 🥩 Thursday — Grilled Steaks with Potatoes, Peppers & Cilantro Sauce

> *Ambitious grilling night. 87°F — take advantage of it.*

### 1. Preparation Upgrade — Reverse-Sear on the Grill
Salt steaks heavily 1 hour before cooking. Set up a two-zone grill — indirect heat on one side, blazing coals on the other. Cook steaks on the cool side with the lid on until internal temp hits 115°F (~20–25 minutes for 1.5-inch steaks). Then move to the searing-hot side for 60–90 seconds per side until you get a dark, mahogany crust. Rest 10 minutes.

**Why:** Reverse-searing gives you edge-to-edge medium-rare with a crust that rivals a steakhouse. The low-then-high approach dries the surface for better Maillard browning. This is the single biggest upgrade you can make to a grilled steak.

### 2. Sauces & Finishing — Punchy Cilantro Chimichurri
Pulse in a food processor (leave it chunky, not smooth): 1 cup packed cilantro (stems and all), ½ cup packed parsley, 4 garlic cloves, 1 minced serrano chile (seeded for Elsie's portion), ¼ cup red wine vinegar, ¾ cup olive oil, 1 teaspoon dried oregano, and flaky salt. Make it 30 minutes ahead.

**Why:** The raw garlic and vinegar cut through beef fat, the herbs add freshness, and the olive oil carries it all. Using cilantro as the lead herb gives it a more vibrant, punchy character that pairs with the peppers.

### 3. Temperature Contrast — Hot Grilled Veg, Cold Chimichurri
Grill the peppers and halved baby potatoes (tossed in oil and salt) directly on the grates alongside the steak during the searing phase. Serve sizzling-hot vegetables with cold chimichurri spooned over everything at the table.

**Why:** Hot, smoky, charred vegetables meeting bright, cold, acidic chimichurri — this is the contrast that makes outdoor grilling feel like an event. The potatoes get a crust from the grill grates that no oven can match.

**🍽️ Plating:** Slice the steak against the grain in thick strips and fan across a warm platter. Pile the grilled peppers and potatoes alongside. Drizzle chimichurri over the steak and serve the rest in a bowl — people always want more. Enjoy that 87°F evening.

## 🍕 Friday — Pizza Night (Guests: Sophia & Faran)

> *⚠️ ABSOLUTELY NO PORK. Creative, impressive, interactive.*

### 1. Preparation Upgrade — Maximum Oven Heat
Crank your oven as high as it goes (500–550°F) with a baking steel or inverted sheet pan on the lowest rack for **at least 45 minutes** before baking. Stretch dough on a well-floured peel and work fast — each pizza should take only 6–8 minutes.

**Why:** Most home pizza fails because the oven isn't hot enough. A preheated steel gives you the leopard-spotted char and puffy cornicione of a pizzeria. This single change transforms home pizza.

### 2. Sauces & Finishing — Three Creative No-Pork Pizzas
1. **Spicy Chicken & Roasted Red Pepper** — shredded rotisserie chicken, roasted red peppers, thin red onion, mozzarella, finished with hot honey drizzle and fresh basil
2. **White Pizza with Ricotta & Greens** — dollops of ricotta, garlic oil base (no red sauce), sautéed spinach or arugula added after baking, lemon zest, black pepper, shaved Parm
3. **Margherita Plus** — San Marzano sauce, fresh mozzarella, fresh basil, plus torn pieces of burrata after baking and a drizzle of really good olive oil

**Why:** Three distinct pizzas give guests variety and make it feel like a pizza party. The hot honey on chicken pizza is the unexpected hit everyone will talk about. The white pizza is elegant. The Margherita Plus with burrata is indulgent. Zero pork, zero compromise.

### 3. Acid & Brightness — Peppery Arugula Salad
Baby arugula, shaved Parmesan, lemon juice, good olive oil, flaky salt. Toss at the last second so the arugula stays perky. For Elsie, add cherry tomatoes and a milder dressing.

**Why:** Pizza is rich, cheesy, and carb-heavy — a peppery, lemony arugula salad is the classic Italian counterpunch. It cleanses the palate between slices.

**🍽️ Plating:** Set up a pizza station: bring each pie to the table on a wooden board, cut at the table. Let guests grab slices with the arugula salad already dressed and waiting. Put out red pepper flakes, oregano, and hot honey as condiments. Make it interactive — Sophia and Faran will love it.

## 🍔 Saturday — Grilled Burgers with Green Goddess Salad

> *Peak summer Saturday energy. 88°F. Cold drinks. Hot grill.*

### 1. Preparation Upgrade — Surface-Season, One-Flip Method
Use 80/20 ground chuck. Season only the outside — mix salt, pepper, and garlic powder, then press onto the formed patties' surfaces. Form patties slightly wider than your buns with a thumbprint dimple in the center. Grill over high direct heat: 4 minutes first side (don't touch), flip once, add cheese immediately, close lid for 2 minutes.

**Why:** Mixing seasoning into burger meat compacts it into a hockey puck. Surface seasoning keeps the interior loose and juicy while building a savory crust. The dimple prevents puffing. One flip only — every flip costs you crust.

### 2. Sauces & Finishing — Burger Sauce + Toasted Buns
Mix: ¼ cup mayo, 2 tablespoons ketchup, 1 tablespoon yellow mustard, 2 tablespoons finely diced dill pickles, 1 teaspoon pickle brine, a pinch of garlic powder, and a few dashes of hot sauce. **Toast the buns cut-side down on the grill for 30 seconds** — this is non-negotiable.

**Why:** The pickle brine is the secret ingredient that makes people ask what's different. Toasted buns create a barrier against burger juice so the bottom bun doesn't disintegrate. This is the difference between a backyard burger and a *great* backyard burger.

### 3. Texture Play — Fresh Green Goddess Salad
Blend: 1 avocado, ½ cup buttermilk, a big handful each of basil, chives, and tarragon, 2 anchovy fillets, juice of 1 lemon, 1 garlic clove, salt and pepper. Toss with crisp Little Gem or romaine, shaved cucumber, snap peas, and radishes. Top with toasted pepitas.

**Why:** Fresh Green Goddess is a revelation — creamy, bright, herbaceous. The toasted pepitas add nutty crunch against the creamy dressing. At 88°F, this cold salad will be the hero of the meal.

**🍽️ Plating:** Set up a burger bar on the patio table: platter of grilled patties with melted cheese, toasted buns, burger sauce in a squeeze bottle, sliced tomato, lettuce, pickles, and onion. Green Goddess salad in a big bowl. Let everyone build their own. Put out a cooler of cold drinks. Peak summer Saturday energy — enjoy that 88°F.
