A 19th-century Russian aristocratic classic, reimagined with both cremini and oyster mushrooms. Sirloin strips seared hard in a screaming-hot pan, then braised with shallots and a splash of brandy until the kitchen fills with that unmistakable stroganoff perfume — rich, earthy, slightly boozy. **Nutrition:** Good iron from beef; mushrooms add B vitamins and selenium.



##### Chef's Tips
- **PREPARATION:** Sear the beef like you mean it — Slice sirloin thin against the grain. Get your cast-iron screaming hot — just past the smoke point of canola oil. Sear in small batches, 45 seconds per side, until you have a hard Maillard crust. Pull immediately. The beef finishes cooking when it goes back into the sauce for the last 90 seconds. - **SAUCE:** Build a proper brandy-fond pan sauce — After searing the beef, sauté cremini and oyster mushrooms in the same pan without moving them for 3-4 minutes until deeply golden. Kill the heat, wait 30 seconds, then fold in full-fat sour cream and a teaspoon of Dijon. The sauce must never boil after the sour cream goes in — above 180°F and it breaks.
- **ACID:** Lemon zest and chive finish — Right before plating, zest half a lemon directly over the stroganoff and scatter a generous handful of finely sliced fresh chives. The zest goes on last — heat destroys its volatile oils.

#### Dinner: Babo Pasta — Homemade Spinach Pasta with Alfredo Sauce

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cuisine** | Italian |
| **Key Ingredients** | spinach, egg yolks, 00 flour, butter, Parmigiano-Reggiano, broccoli |
| **Source** | [Family Recipe](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/fresh-pasta-dough) |
The family's signature dish — handmade spinach pasta using only egg yolks for a rich, golden dough that practically glows on the plate. Tossed in a simple Alfredo of butter, Parmigiano-Reggiano, and starchy pasta water, with blanched broccoli florets folded in at the last second. Fresh pasta cooks in barely two minutes, so dinner comes together faster than you'd expect.

*Mix the dough before swim lessons, let it rest wrapped in plastic for 30 minutes. Roll and cut when you get home — Elsie loves cranking the pasta machine.*

**Nutrition:** Eggs provide protein and choline; spinach adds iron and folate; broccoli for vitamin C.



##### Chef's Tips
- **SAUCE:** True Alfredo — emulsify, don't cook — Melt 4 tablespoons of unsalted butter in a wide skillet over medium-low heat. Add the hot, just-drained pasta directly into the butter with 1/2 cup of starchy pasta water. Toss vigorously. Kill the heat. Add 2 cups of finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano in three additions, tossing constantly between each. The pasta water's starch and the fat from the butter create an emulsion — a glossy, clinging sauce with no cream needed. If it tightens, add another splash of pasta water. If it breaks, a tablespoon of cold butter whisked in saves it.
- **TEXTURE:** Toasted garlic breadcrumb gremolata — In a small skillet, warm 2 tablespoons of good olive oil over medium heat. Add 3 cloves of thinly sliced garlic and cook until just golden — 90 seconds, no more. Add 1/2 cup coarse fresh breadcrumbs (tear day-old bread, don't use the canister stuff). Toast until deeply golden and crunchy, about 3 minutes. Toss with lemon zest and a pinch of flaky salt. Scatter generously over each plate of pasta.
- **PREPARATION:** Rest and hydrate the pasta dough — After mixing the spinach pasta dough, wrap it tightly in plastic and rest at room temperature for a full 30 minutes. The gluten relaxes, making it easier to roll thin without tearing. When rolling, go to the second-thinnest setting on your pasta machine — thin enough to be translucent but sturdy enough to hold the sauce. Blanch the spinach, squeeze it bone-dry in a towel, and chop it fine before incorporating. Any residual water makes the dough sticky and impossible to work.

#### Dinner: Beef & Broccoli Stir Fry with Oyster Mushrooms

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cuisine** | Chinese |
| **Key Ingredients** | flank steak, broccoli, oyster mushrooms, garlic, ginger, green onions, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine |
| **Source** | [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/chinese-american-beef-and-broccoli-with-oyster-sauce-recipe) |
A Chinese-American classic that dates to the 1920s, when Cantonese immigrants swapped hard-to-find gai lan for Western broccoli and created something entirely new. Velveted flank steak wok-seared at screaming heat with broccoli and oyster mushrooms, glazed in a sauce of oyster sauce, soy, garlic, ginger, and Shaoxing wine. Thirty minutes, start to plate.

*Velvet the beef in a cornstarch slurry for 15 minutes before cooking — this is the secret to takeout-tender strips at home.*

**Nutrition:** Beef provides iron and B12; broccoli adds vitamin C to aid iron absorption; mushrooms for selenium.



##### Chef's Tips
- **MARINADE:** Velvet the flank steak — Slice flank steak thin against the grain. Toss with 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 tablespoon cornstarch, 1/2 teaspoon baking soda, 1 tablespoon Shaoxing wine, and 1 teaspoon neutral oil. Let it sit for 25 minutes. The baking soda raises the pH, the cornstarch forms a protective sheath, and the wine tenderizes. When this hits a scorching-hot wok, the exterior sears while the interior stays impossibly tender. This is the single technique that separates home stir fry from restaurant stir fry.
- **PREPARATION:** Blanch the broccoli, char the mushrooms — Blanch broccoli florets in boiling salted water for exactly 60 seconds, then plunge into ice water. Drain and pat dry. For the oyster mushrooms: tear them into large pieces (don't cut — torn edges sear better), then cook in a dry wok over maximum heat without touching them for 2 minutes until charred on one side. Remove. Everything gets combined at the very end with the sauce. Stir fry is an assembly job — every component is cooked separately at its ideal heat.
- **SAUCE:** Glossy Shaoxing-sesame finishing sauce — Mix 2 tablespoons oyster sauce, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 tablespoon Shaoxing wine, 1 teaspoon dark soy sauce (for color), 1 teaspoon sugar, and 1 teaspoon cornstarch dissolved in 2 tablespoons water. After all components are seared, return everything to the wok over high heat, pour in the sauce, and toss for 30 seconds until glossy and clinging. Finish with a drizzle of toasted sesame oil off the heat — never cook sesame oil, it burns and turns bitter.

#### Dinner: Pan-Seared Branzino with Lemon-Herb Butter & Roasted Broccoli

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cuisine** | Mediterranean |
| **Key Ingredients** | branzino fillets, butter, lemon, capers, fresh parsley, broccoli, olive oil |
| **Source** | [Food & Wine](https://www.foodandwine.com/recipes/pan-roasted-branzino-with-caper-butter) |
Skin-on branzino fillets seared in a cast-iron pan until the skin crackles like a chip, finished with a sauce of brown butter, lemon, capers, and fresh herbs. Branzino — a Mediterranean sea bass prized for its mild, sweet flesh — needs almost nothing beyond high heat, good butter, and restraint. Served alongside simple roasted broccoli with lemon zest and flaky salt.

*Pat the fillets bone-dry with paper towels before they hit the pan — wet skin steams instead of crisping. Dad's fish craving, answered.*

##### Chef's Tips
- **PREPARATION:** Achieve glass-crispy skin — Pat the fillets aggressively dry with paper towels — both sides, twice. Score the skin with 3 shallow diagonal cuts to prevent curling. Season the skin side with salt 5 minutes before cooking to draw out residual moisture. Heat a stainless steel or cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat with a thin film of high-smoke-point oil (grapeseed or canola). #### Dinner: BBQ Pork Butt with Broccoli Slaw

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cuisine** | American BBQ |
| **Key Ingredients** | pork butt, smoked paprika, brown sugar, broccoli stems, cabbage, apple cider vinegar, brioche buns |
| **Source** | [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-complete-guide-to-smoking-pulled-pork-barbecue) |
Low-and-slow American barbecue at its finest: a pork butt rubbed with smoked paprika, brown sugar, mustard powder, and black pepper, cooked for hours until it falls apart at the touch of a fork. The tradition stretches back to colonial Southern pit cooks who transformed tough shoulder cuts into something transcendent through patience and smoke. Piled on brioche buns with a crunchy broccoli slaw made from all those stems you've been saving.

*Start the pork butt by 9 AM for a 6 PM dinner. The broccoli slaw uses stems from the week — zero waste, maximum crunch.*

**Nutrition:** Pork provides protein, thiamine, and zinc; cabbage slaw adds vitamin C and fiber.



##### Chef's Tips
- **MARINADE:** Dry brine overnight, rub in the morning — The night before, coat the pork butt generously with kosher salt — about 1 tablespoon per 5 pounds. Let it sit uncovered in the fridge on a wire rack for 12-24 hours. The salt penetrates deep, seasons the meat throughout, and the uncovered fridge time dries the surface — which is how you get bark. In the morning, apply your rub: smoked paprika, brown sugar, black pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, a pinch of cayenne. The sugar in the rub caramelizes against the dry, salted surface and forms the crust that everyone fights over.
- **PREPARATION:** Apple cider vinegar spritz and the stall — Smoke at 225-250°F with a mix of hickory and apple wood. Every 45 minutes after the first 2 hours, spritz the surface with a 50/50 mix of apple cider vinegar and apple juice using a spray bottle. When the internal temp hits 165°F and stalls, resist the urge to crank the heat. Wrap in butcher paper (not foil — foil steams and softens the bark). Continue to 203°F internal, then rest wrapped in a cooler with towels for at least 1 hour. The carry-over cooking and juice redistribution during the rest is what turns tough collagen into silk.
- **ACID:** Upgrade the broccoli slaw with mustard vinaigrette — Shred broccoli stems on a box grater or mandoline. Toss with shredded green cabbage, shredded carrots, and thinly sliced scallions. For the dressing: whisk 3 tablespoons apple cider vinegar, 1 tablespoon whole-grain mustard, 1 tablespoon honey, 2 tablespoons olive oil, and a generous pinch of celery seed. Dress the slaw 15 minutes before serving — no earlier, or it goes limp. Top with toasted sunflower seeds for crunch.

**Dressing:** Blood orange vinaigrette: fresh blood orange juice, white wine vinegar, Dijon mustard, extra-virgin olive oil, minced shallot, honey, flaky salt, cracked black pepper

**Why these flavors work:** The char on roasted broccoli provides nutty bitterness that blood orange tames with tart sweetness. Burrata adds lush creaminess. Italian amaro-dolce principles at work.



### Chopped Broccoli, Kale & Apple Salad with Cheddar Croutons *(Tossed Dinner)*

Finely chopped raw broccoli, massaged lacinato kale, crisp Honeycrisp apple matchsticks, toasted sunflower seeds, dried cranberries, and sharp cheddar croutons.



**Dressing:** Apple cider vinaigrette: apple cider vinegar, Dijon mustard, maple syrup, extra-virgin olive oil, minced shallot, salt, black pepper

**Why these flavors work:** Raw broccoli crunch pairs with tender massaged kale. Apple sweetness and cranberry tartness play against sharp cheddar croutons. Maple-mustard vinaigrette unifies with autumnal warmth.



### Asian Broccoli Slaw with Sesame-Ginger Dressing *(Side)*

Shredded broccoli stems and florets, shaved carrots, red cabbage, and edamame tossed in a bright sesame-ginger dressing. Topped with crispy wonton strips.



**Dressing:** Sesame-ginger dressing: toasted sesame oil, rice vinegar, fresh grated ginger, soy sauce, honey, neutral oil, lime juice, garlic, salt

**Why these flavors work:** Nutty sesame oil grounds bright rice vinegar and lime acid. Fresh ginger adds heat. Transforms raw cruciferous crunch into something addictive.



### Shaved Fennel, Radicchio & Citrus Salad with Marcona Almonds *(Side)*

Paper-thin fennel, torn radicchio, navel orange supremes, and a scattering of Marcona almonds. A palate cleanser that cuts through rich mains.



**Dressing:** Lemon-shallot vinaigrette: fresh lemon juice, finely minced shallot, champagne vinegar, extra-virgin olive oil, Dijon mustard, flaky salt, cracked black pepper

**Why these flavors work:** Fennel anise sweetness tames radicchio bitterness. Citrus adds juicy brightness. Marcona almonds bring buttery richness and crunch. Classic bitter-sweet-acid Mediterranean triad.

## Nutrition Summary

This week leans hearty and comforting — exactly right for a family bridging the last cold snap of winter with the first real taste of spring. You're getting excellent protein variety across beef, ham, fish, and pork, plus a serious vitamin C and K boost from all that nightly broccoli. The gaps are real but fixable: no whole grains at dinner, mostly green vegetables, and the preparations skew rich. Balance it out by weaving whole wheat bread into lunches, adding Greek yogurt and fruit at snack time for Elsie's growing bones, and tossing some hummus and carrots into lunchboxes. Friday's branzino delivers a welcome omega-3 boost, and a few handfuls of walnuts or chia seeds during the week round things out. Enjoy those buttery gratins and silky Alfredo without guilt — the lunches and snacks are doing the nutritional heavy lifting.
