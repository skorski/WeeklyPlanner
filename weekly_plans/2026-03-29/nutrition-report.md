## Why These Nutrients Matter

A deeper look at the vitamins, minerals, and nutrients this week's plan targets — what they do, what happens without enough, and where to find them.

### Fiber

Fiber is the unsung hero of digestion, heart health, and blood sugar control. It feeds the good bacteria in your gut (the microbiome), keeps you feeling full longer, and helps prevent the energy crashes that come after refined-carb meals — which is especially relevant this week. For Elsie, fiber supports healthy digestion and steady energy for school and sports.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Constipation, blood sugar spikes and crashes, feeling hungry again soon after eating, sluggish digestion, and long-term increased risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 25-35g | Elsie: 20-25g |
| **Best Food Sources** | Lentils (15g per cup), black beans (15g), raspberries (8g per cup), avocado (10g), whole-grain bread (3g per slice), sweet potatoes (4g), edamame (8g per cup), chia seeds (10g per oz) |

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids (DHA, EPA, ALA)

Omega-3s are essential fats your body can't make on its own. They reduce inflammation, support brain development (critical for Elsie at age 8), protect heart health, and may improve mood and focus. DHA is particularly important for children's cognitive development and learning. For active adults, omega-3s speed muscle recovery and reduce joint inflammation.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Dry skin, poor concentration, joint stiffness, mood swings, slow wound healing. In children: difficulty focusing in school, dry/rough skin patches. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 250-500mg EPA+DHA | Elsie: 200-250mg DHA |
| **Best Food Sources** | Salmon (2,260mg per 3oz), sardines (1,350mg), canned albacore tuna (1,000mg per can), walnuts (2,570mg ALA per oz), chia seeds (5,060mg ALA per oz), flaxseed (6,390mg ALA per tbsp), edamame (300mg ALA per cup) |

### Calcium

Calcium builds and maintains strong bones and teeth — a non-negotiable for Elsie, whose bones are still growing rapidly at age 8. Peak bone mass is built during childhood and adolescence, so the calcium she eats now literally determines her bone strength for life. For adults, calcium also supports muscle contraction, nerve signaling, and blood clotting.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Muscle cramps, tingling in fingers, brittle nails. Long-term: osteoporosis, stress fractures in active individuals, dental problems. Children may show delayed growth. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 1,000mg | Elsie: 1,300mg (higher than adults because she's growing!) |
| **Best Food Sources** | Greek yogurt (200mg per cup), milk (300mg per cup), mozzarella (222mg per oz), cheddar (200mg per oz), fortified orange juice (350mg per cup), canned sardines with bones (325mg), kale (94mg per cup cooked), tahini (64mg per tbsp), chia seeds (63mg per tbsp) |

### Iron

Iron carries oxygen in your blood to every cell in your body. Without enough iron, you feel exhausted, foggy, and cold. For an active family, iron is the difference between crushing a workout and feeling like you're running through mud. It's especially critical for the adult woman (menstrual blood loss increases iron needs) and for Elsie (growing bodies need iron for brain development and growth).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Fatigue that doesn't improve with sleep, pale skin, feeling cold all the time, brittle nails, dizziness, poor concentration in school (for Elsie), frequent colds/illness. |
| **Daily Target** | Wife: 18mg | Husband: 8mg | Elsie: 10mg |
| **Best Food Sources** | Red meat (2.7mg per 3oz — best absorbed), lentils (6.6mg per cup), spinach (6.4mg per cup cooked), dark chocolate 70%+ (3.4mg per oz), edamame (3.5mg per cup), fortified cereals (18mg per serving), chicken thigh (1.1mg per 3oz). Pair with vitamin C to boost plant-iron absorption by up to 6x. |

### Vitamin D

Vitamin D is the 'sunshine vitamin' that helps your body absorb calcium — without it, all that yogurt and cheese is far less effective. It also supports immune function (crucial as spring allergies hit), regulates mood, and reduces inflammation. In late March in Cleveland, the sun angle is still recovering from winter — your family is very likely vitamin D insufficient right now.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Fatigue, bone pain, frequent illness/colds, muscle weakness, low mood/winter blues that linger into spring. In children: slow growth, bone pain, increased susceptibility to respiratory infections. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 600 IU (15mcg) | Elsie: 600 IU (15mcg) — many experts recommend 1,000-2,000 IU |
| **Best Food Sources** | Salmon (570 IU per 3oz), canned tuna (40 IU per 3oz), fortified milk (115 IU per cup), fortified orange juice (100 IU per cup), egg yolks (44 IU each), fortified yogurt (80 IU per cup), mushrooms exposed to UV light (400 IU per cup). Supplement strongly recommended for all three family members in late winter/early spring. |

### Potassium

Potassium is sodium's counterpart — it helps your body flush excess sodium and maintain healthy blood pressure. This week, with the sodium load from sausage, pizza (×2), and the Saturday burger blowout, potassium is especially important. It also prevents muscle cramps during activity, supports nerve function, and maintains fluid balance.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Muscle cramps (especially at night or during exercise), fatigue, weakness, bloating/water retention, constipation, irregular heartbeat in severe cases. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 2,600-3,400mg | Elsie: 2,300mg |
| **Best Food Sources** | Sweet potato (541mg per medium), banana (422mg), avocado (485mg), black beans (305mg per half cup), lentils (731mg per cup), spinach (839mg per cup cooked), yogurt (380mg per cup), cantaloupe (427mg per cup), edamame (676mg per cup) |

### Folate (Vitamin B9)

Folate is essential for DNA synthesis and cell division — it's the nutrient that helps your body make new cells. For the adult woman, folate is always important (critical if pregnancy is ever a possibility). For Elsie, it supports her rapid growth and development. Folate also helps form red blood cells, preventing a type of anemia that causes extreme fatigue.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Extreme tiredness, mouth sores, gray hair, poor growth in children, difficulty concentrating, irritability. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 400mcg DFE | Elsie: 300mcg DFE |
| **Best Food Sources** | Lentils (358mcg per cup — 90% DV in one serving!), edamame (482mcg per cup), black beans (128mcg per half cup), spinach (263mcg per cup cooked), asparagus (134mcg per half cup), fortified cereals (100-400mcg per serving), avocado (81mcg per half) |

### Magnesium

Magnesium is involved in over 300 enzyme reactions in your body — it's the behind-the-scenes mineral that makes everything work. For this active family, magnesium is crucial for muscle function (preventing cramps and soreness), sleep quality, and stress management. It also helps regulate blood sugar after those carb-heavy dinners.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Muscle cramps and twitches, trouble sleeping, anxiety/restlessness, headaches, chocolate cravings (yes, really — the body craves magnesium-rich dark chocolate). |
| **Daily Target** | Wife: 310mg | Husband: 400mg | Elsie: 130mg |
| **Best Food Sources** | Pumpkin seeds (156mg per oz — the single best source), dark chocolate 70%+ (65mg per oz), almonds (76mg per oz), edamame (99mg per cup), black beans (60mg per half cup), spinach (157mg per cup cooked), avocado (58mg per half), banana (32mg) |

### Vitamin C

Beyond immune support, vitamin C is a powerful antioxidant and the key to unlocking plant-based iron. It can increase iron absorption from plant foods by up to 6 times — which matters enormously this week because the best iron sources in your lunches and snacks (lentils, edamame, hummus, spinach) are all plant-based. It also supports collagen production for healthy skin and joint recovery after activity.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Deficiency Signs** | Slow wound healing, bleeding gums, frequent colds that last longer than they should, dry/rough skin, easy bruising, fatigue. |
| **Daily Target** | Adults: 75-90mg | Elsie: 25mg |
| **Best Food Sources** | Bell peppers (80-120mg per medium pepper — more than an orange!), strawberries (85mg per cup), clementines (35mg each), broccoli (51mg per half cup cooked), kiwi (64mg each), tomatoes (20mg per medium), lemon juice (31mg per half lemon) |

### Vitamin A

Vitamin A supports vision (especially night vision), immune function, and healthy skin. It's also essential for growth in children — Elsie needs it for everything from her eyesight to her immune system. The good news: vitamin A is easy to get from orange and dark green vegetables, many of which show up in the recommended lunches.

| | |
