#### Spring Niçoise Salad with Seared Tuna, Asparagus & Radishes *(Composed)*

**Cuisine:** French
A composed arrangement of seared rare tuna, blanched asparagus, halved soft-boiled eggs, fingerling potatoes, spring radishes, Niçoise olives, and haricots verts on a bed of butter lettuce — each element placed with intention, not tossed.

**Key Ingredients:** sushi-grade tuna steaks, asparagus, soft-boiled eggs, fingerling potatoes, spring radishes, Niçoise olives, haricots verts, cherry tomatoes, capers

**Dressing:** Anchovy-shallot vinaigrette: extra-virgin olive oil, red wine vinegar, Dijon mustard, minced shallot, 2 anchovy fillets (mashed), fresh lemon juice, minced garlic, fresh thyme leaves, flaky sea salt, cracked black pepper


**Why these flavors work:** The briny anchovy vinaigrette ties together the richness of seared tuna and soft eggs with the bright snap of spring asparagus and peppery radishes. Each composed element offers a different texture — creamy potato, crisp haricots verts, silky tuna — following the French principle of composed contrast where every forkful is a new combination.


🌿 *Asparagus and radishes hit peak season on the East Coast in late March through April. Fingerling potatoes are arriving at farmers' markets. This is the definitive spring Niçoise.*

**Source:** [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-salade-nicoise-recipe)
*Composed dinner salad — a complete, elegant meal on a plate. Spring asparagus and radishes replace winter elements for a seasonal update. Substantial enough for a standalone dinner with multiple proteins.*


#### Green Goddess Salad with Herbs & Cashew Dressing *(Tossed)*

**Cuisine:** American
A crunchy, vibrant tossed salad of finely chopped green cabbage, Persian cucumbers, and scallions drenched in a thick blended green goddess dressing made with fresh basil, spinach, cashews, Parmesan, and lemon. Top with grilled chicken or crispy chickpeas for a full meal.

**Key Ingredients:** green cabbage, Persian cucumbers, scallions, chives, fresh basil, baby spinach, raw cashews, lemon juice, rice vinegar, garlic, Parmesan, shallot

**Dressing:** Green goddess blender dressing: 1 cup packed fresh basil leaves, 1 cup baby spinach, ¼ cup raw cashews, 2 cloves garlic, 1 small shallot, juice of 2 lemons, ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil, ⅓ cup finely grated Parmesan, 2 tablespoons rice vinegar, 1 teaspoon kosher salt — blend until thick, smooth, and vibrantly green


**Why these flavors work:** The dressing is the star — creamy cashews and salty Parmesan provide body and umami, while basil and spinach deliver herbaceous brightness. Lemon and rice vinegar cut through the richness with sharp acidity. Finely chopping all vegetables creates a slaw-like texture that holds the thick dressing in every single crunchy bite.


🌿 *Fresh basil and spring herbs are beginning to appear at East Coast markets in late March. The cabbage and cucumber base keeps it crisp and substantial year-round, but the herb-forward dressing shines brightest with just-picked basil.*

**Source:** [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/article/green-goddess-salad/)
*User-requested craving! The modern viral green goddess salad as a tossed dinner — thick herby dressing, maximum crunch. Pairs with any protein from the dinner list or stands alone as a light spring dinner.*

#### Shaved Asparagus & Pea Shoot Salad with Lemon-Parmesan Vinaigrette
**Cuisine:** Italian
Paper-thin ribbons of raw asparagus and delicate pea shoots tossed with shaved Parmesan, toasted pine nuts, and a bright lemony vinaigrette. An elegant raw preparation that showcases early spring produce at its absolute freshest.

**Key Ingredients:** asparagus (shaved raw with peeler), pea shoots, Parmesan (shaved), toasted pine nuts, lemon, extra-virgin olive oil, Dijon mustard

**Dressing:** Lemon-Parmesan vinaigrette: 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice, 1 teaspoon lemon zest, ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil, 2 tablespoons finely grated Parmesan, 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard, 1 small clove garlic (minced), ½ teaspoon honey, flaky sea salt, cracked black pepper


**Why these flavors work:** Raw asparagus is nutty and sweet — nothing like its cooked counterpart. The lemon-Parmesan vinaigrette brings bright acidity and savory umami that amplify asparagus's natural grassy flavor. Pine nuts add buttery richness and pea shoots contribute delicate spring freshness. This is the Italian crudo principle — trusting the raw ingredient to be the star.


🌿 *Asparagus is THE marquee spring vegetable on the East Coast, peaking late March through May. Pea shoots are among the very first spring greens at farmers' markets — tender, sweet, and ephemeral.*

**Source:** [Love and Lemons](https://www.loveandlemons.com/asparagus-salad/)
*Side salad celebrating peak spring asparagus in its purest raw form. Pairs beautifully with the scallops, risotto, orecchiette, or any Italian dinner. Stunning on the plate with minimal effort.*


#### Quick-Pickled Radish & Cucumber Salad with Sesame-Ginger Dressing
**Cuisine:** Japanese-inspired
Thinly sliced spring radishes and Persian cucumbers quick-pickled in rice vinegar, then dressed with a nutty sesame-ginger dressing and topped with toasted sesame seeds and thinly sliced scallions. Bright, crunchy, and palate-cleansing.

**Key Ingredients:** spring radishes, Persian cucumbers, rice vinegar, toasted sesame oil, fresh ginger, toasted sesame seeds, scallions, soy sauce, lime juice

**Dressing:** Sesame-ginger dressing: 2 tablespoons toasted sesame oil, 2 tablespoons rice vinegar, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 tablespoon freshly grated ginger, 1 small clove garlic (minced), 1 teaspoon honey, juice of ½ lime, 1 tablespoon toasted white sesame seeds


**Why these flavors work:** The quick pickle softens the radishes' sharp bite while preserving their crunch. Sesame oil adds warm nuttiness, fresh ginger brings zing, and the rice vinegar–soy sauce combination delivers that addictive sweet-salty-sour balance. This is a palate cleanser — designed to cut through rich mains like jambalaya, kofta, or steak.


🌿 *Spring radishes are at their best in late March on the East Coast — sweeter, crisper, and less fiery than midsummer radishes. Their pink-red color against white cucumber makes this salad pop visually.*

**Source:** [Food52](https://food52.com/recipes/80043-japanese-cucumber-salad)
*Asian-inspired side salad that naturally pairs with the Korean, Japanese, Thai, and Vietnamese dinners. Spring radishes showcase the season while the quick-pickle method delivers maximum flavor with zero cooking.*
