## And Finally… The Island Where People Talk by Whistling

On the tiny Canary Island of La Gomera, people don't shout across the steep ravines — they whistle. Silbo Gomero is a fully developed whistled language that compresses Spanish into just six whistle sounds (two vowels, four consonants) and carries conversations up to three miles across mountain valleys. Shepherds use it to coordinate flocks. Villagers use it to announce news. Kids learn it in school — yes, there are whistling exams. It's the loudest sound the human mouth can make, turned into grammar. UNESCO declared it Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009, which is a fancy way of saying: please never stop doing this extremely cool thing.
